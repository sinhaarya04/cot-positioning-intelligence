# Literature review: public news and the remaining value of COT disclosure

**Review date: 19 October 2026 (America/New_York). Research assessment, not an empirical result or a claim of priority.**

## Recommendation

Develop a study of **whether public news arriving during the reporting lag substitutes for or complements the eventual position disclosure**. At one common post-publication forecast origin, compare earlier and updated public predictors, each with and without the same newly reported managed-money statistic. Hold the contract, future outcome, scale, training sample and forecast date fixed. The primary contrast is the difference between the report's incremental risk-forecast contribution under these two public information sets.

Separately test whether the updates improve pre-release reconstruction of the same Tuesday position. Improved reconstructibility does not imply reduced risk relevance. The intended empirical contribution is to distinguish these two properties and determine whether a measurable report contribution survives the intervening information. These are competing hypotheses, not an assumed finding of information decay.

This strengthens the earlier substitution-only proposal: avoiding use of a freely available report was not a compelling economic motivation, and combining reconstruction with quantile forecasting was too close to existing work. Four-model information comparisons are themselves standard. The contribution must come from a robust and useful empirical finding, not the comparison's name or an extra feature.

The CFTC's [May 2026 consultation](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-05/pdf/2026-08743.pdf), questions 1, 2 and 6–7, explicitly raises publication frequency and the three-business-day lag. This motivates the issue; the proposed observational forecast comparison cannot establish the effect of changing that policy.

The focused literature review leaves this precise experiment plausibly distinct but does not establish priority. **Proskurin remains the main unresolved full-text gate**, particularly because its editorial summary already compares Tuesday and Friday availability. No research experiments were run during this revision.

## Contribution test and reviewer objections

The [working paper](working-paper.md) now defines E0/E1 using early public predictors without/with the newly released statistic, and L0/L1 using updated predictors without/with it. Every model is issued after publication. With common loss `L`, the primary contrast is `H = [L(E0)-L(E1)] - [L(L0)-L(L1)]`; the companion estimate is the remaining gain `V_L = L(L0)-L(L1)`. The reconstruction-error improvement is an independent diagnostic, not an input to these four risk models.

| Likely objection | Required answer in the design and evidence |
|---|---|
| “You combined Boos–Grob with residual forecasting and quantiles.” | The main question concerns how a report's marginal contribution changes with intervening public information. The primary contrast uses direct predictors and does not require a reconstruction model. Reconstruction is evidence about inferability, not the source of the novelty claim. |
| “Proskurin already compared Tuesday and Friday.” | Keep the forecast origin and future outcome identical in all four cells; change only the declared predictors. An early-information model receiving the Friday report is explicitly a restriction at Friday's forecast origin, never a feasible Tuesday trade. Verify Proskurin's full design before claiming this distinction survives. |
| “A delay obviously makes information less useful.” | Predictive complementarity is also possible: intervening news can make an older position statistic more relevant to subsequent risk. Test both directions and report the component gains. Do not equate a calendar delay with economic irrelevance. |
| “A weak public model creates apparent report value.” | Require strong direct momentum/volatility/inventory predictors and a prespecified nonlinear sensitivity permitting interactions in every appropriate model. A result that disappears with a credible public baseline cannot support the main claim. |
| “Your four cells do not identify causal learning.” | Agree. They estimate forecast usefulness under explicit information restrictions. Reconstruction improvement and reduced report value are consistent with redundancy, not proof of a causal pathway or a disclosure-policy effect. |
| “No significant improvement means the report is useless.” | Require a useful-effect margin and sufficiently tight uncertainty for the remaining report contribution. An imprecise null is inconclusive; other report fields, decisions and immediate announcement effects remain outside scope. |
| “You selected an interesting crisis or commodity afterward.” | Freeze two markets, one horizon, two equally weighted tails and one primary contrast. Keep uncertainty regimes, physical tightness, new trader groups and profit claims outside the primary design. |

A persuasive result would quantify a stable change in report usefulness across credible public models, establish whether a practically meaningful contribution remains, and show how that relates to reconstruction accuracy. It could support an applied information-value paper. It would not become a methodological or causal contribution merely because the estimates are significant.

**The strongest objection is now clear.** A reviewer can combine Boos–Grob's reconstruction, Wang's unexpected positions, Kang's residual return regressions and Pradkhan's quantiles and ask whether we have merely assembled familiar tools. A new model name, a different commodity, or a significant residual coefficient would not answer that objection. The contribution must be evidence about how **intervening public information changes the report's usefulness**, with reconstructibility evaluated separately. A position estimate's statistical accuracy and the marginal risk value of the report need not move together.

**Kang's appendix changes the standard we must meet.** Tables IA.V and IA.VI explicitly fit 52-week rolling decompositions using weeks t−51 through t. They then relate momentum/nonmomentum components to later returns, including additional controls and bootstrap inference. We cannot claim earlier studies only used static full-sample residuals. Their current position is included in the decomposition fit; our pre-release replica cannot use that target until after it arrives. That is an implementation distinction supporting the substitution question, not a sufficient standalone novelty claim. The published main article's post-release portfolio table is Table V, not draft Table 4.

**Wang supplies the missing older precedent.** Its expected/unexpected position decomposition is applied to same-week currency volatility. This directly defeats a broad claim to invent “position surprises and risk,” even though our market, forecast timing and loss comparison differ. Retain a simple autoregressive/ARIMA replica among the benchmarks and make the temporal distinction explicit. A statistically unexplained residual is not automatically new information to market participants.

**Keep the proposed contribution narrow and demanding.** Estimate how the report's incremental contribution changes when intervening predictors are admitted, and report the remaining contribution against a useful-effect margin. Show reconstruction accuracy separately on common dates. Neither a good replica nor a significant residual coefficient establishes that the report is redundant. The revised primary contrast does not depend on downstream models trained on generated positions.

**Defer uncertainty regimes.** Wang includes lagged volatility, Kang conditions on commodity volatility, and newer papers study market states. Any future test using earlier reconstruction errors must add beyond ordinary volatility and trend, use only information available beforehand and account for its additional training cost. This is no longer a secondary claim of the primary design.

**Research recommendation:** use the common-origin information comparison above as the main question; retain the earlier reconstruction-substitution comparison as a possible extension. Do not call the research gap confirmed or publication-ready. The specific comparison was not found in this inspected set; absence from a focused set is not proof of priority. Complete Proskurin, resolve the data/power gates and freeze the protocol before experiments. No forecasting experiments were run during this literature follow-up.

## Yu full-text follow-up: what changes in the proposal

**The experiment actually performed.** Sections 2–3 use weekly WTI data for January 1994–June 2021. Hedging pressure is commercial net longs divided by total commercial positions, not managed-money net positions divided by market open interest. Six binary signals compare current pressure with its value 1, 2, 4, 12, 24 and 50 weeks earlier. Each standardized signal is scaled by its return-predictive coefficient; the first principal component becomes HPM–SPCA. Section 2.2 explicitly limits its construction to information available through the forecast date. Return models use 500-week rolling estimation; reported out-of-sample evaluation begins in August 2003. The main score is mean-squared-error reduction against historical-average returns, with Clark–West tests.

**The strongest comparator matters more than the abstract.** Table 5 reports HPM–SPCA's out-of-sample R² as 0.946%. Table 6 reports 1.696% for one-week price momentum alone and 2.006% after adding HPM–SPCA; the incremental test is significant only at the 10% level for this comparator. Combined price momentum likewise rises from 1.155% to 1.427%, with a 10% incremental test. These are percentage reductions in forecast MSE relative to the benchmark, not trading returns. The difference 2.006 − 1.696 = 0.310 percentage points uses the common benchmark denominator; it is not a 0.310% investment gain. HPM–SPCA therefore does not uniformly beat momentum by itself. Adding positioning information to momentum is an existing experiment and cannot be our novelty claim.

**Controls and extensions already covered.** Section 2.3 includes inventory, production, refinery input, open interest, futures–spot basis, spot volatility, S&P 500 returns, TED spread, imports, exports and net imports. Tables 3–6 distinguish univariate, bivariate and PCA/SPCA comparisons; they should not be described as a single unrestricted model containing every control. Sections 4–6 examine sentiment, liquidity and volatility states, forecast combinations, second through fourth nearby contracts, longer horizons, mean–variance allocations and long/short timing. These substantially reduce the novelty of adding inventories, regimes, maturity tests or portfolio usefulness to a COT forecast.

**Timing limits of what can be verified.** Section 2.1 places signals at the end of the week, and section 2.2 states a past-information restriction. The inspected article does not document an actual COT release ledger, holiday/backlog alignment, the exact within-week entry timestamp, or original release vintages. Its weekly EIA price description also does not identify a contract-preserving executable entry/exit rule. This is an unresolved implementation detail, not proof of look-ahead bias. Publication-aware timing was already studied elsewhere and is not by itself our contribution. Data are available on request; no replication data or code were obtained here.

**State results need careful interpretation.** Section 4.2 and Table 9 classify volatility using the full sample, while liquidity uses a historical 50-week median. Full-sample volatility groups are retrospective diagnostics, not an ex-ante uncertainty rule that we can directly adopt. The sentiment index also includes a positioning measure; its predictive relationship is not an independent causal identification of sentiment. Any later uncertainty-group extension must use only earlier reconstruction errors; the present primary design uses no such groups.

**What is absent from this article's experiments.** The SPCA transformation combines observed hedging-pressure signals; it does not reconstruct the not-yet-published position. The article does not compare an official-position forecast with a separately trained pre-release-replica forecast, evaluate the loss from substituting that replica for the disclosure, or test whether this loss varies with ex-ante replica uncertainty. Its primary target is the conditional mean return; the portfolio variance estimate is a rolling historical variance, not a COT-versus-replica quantile comparison. These distinctions establish separation from **Yu**, not universal originality.

**Required design response.** Include release-safe lagged commercial HPM signals and matched price-momentum horizons in the public baseline. Treat an HPM–SPCA mean-return reproduction as a comparator exercise, separate from the primary tail-risk test. Freeze any adaptation to signed prices and past-only scaling before evaluation. A weak momentum baseline would make any apparent disclosure value unconvincing. Also narrow the claim: the current primary `q_t` is one managed-money statistic. Its incremental forecast contribution cannot establish the value of every field in the entire COT release.

**Proskurin request submitted; full text pending:** Oleksandr Proskurin (2021), *Does the CFTC Report Have Predictive Power: Machine Learning Approach*, *The Journal of Financial Data Science* 3(3), 139–151, Summer 2021, DOI **10.3905/jfds.2021.1.065**. The request covers the complete article and any available methodological supplement. The full-paper review remains an experiment gate; the issue introduction cannot substitute for it.

## What evidence would justify the contribution?

1. **A stable interaction between the two information sources.** Estimate the paired four-cell contrast directly, with common dates and joint uncertainty. The same model family must handle both public-only and report-augmented forecasts competently.
2. **A meaningful remaining report contribution or a useful bound.** The magnitude of `V_L` matters alongside the contrast. More news reducing a gain from a negligible amount to another negligible amount would not support a substantial economic conclusion.
3. **A separate account of inferability.** Compare early and late estimates of the same Tuesday position using only earlier disclosed training targets. Report whether reconstruction improves, without treating that pattern as causal mediation or using favorable downstream results to select the replica.
4. **Original availability and values.** Keep observation, public cutoff, release and forecast clocks distinct. A current historical value is not proof of what a user saw in the first release. A daily convention also leaves immediate announcement effects outside the study.
5. **A clear failure rule.** An unstable sign, weak baselines, materially revised data, inadequate precision or an already matching prior study prevents the intended claim. Preserve such findings as an honest pilot rather than searching new regimes for a publishable result.

## Feasibility and stop rules before modeling

- Complete the pending Proskurin full-text review. Published-version checks of Kang and Pradkhan and the main-method reviews of Lin and Ganepola are now complete to the scope recorded above. Any uninspected supplements remain explicitly outside that scope. If Proskurin or another close paper already performs the proposed common-origin information comparison, acknowledge replication and establish a substantively different question before claiming originality.
- Complete a release/value coverage table by unique publication batch, market and vintage class. The current evidence ledger is not an eligible experiment panel. Do not impute scheduled Fridays as verified release times.
- Validate the six-contract Bloomberg pilot, then acquire the longer panel. Repeated contracts on one date are correlated exposures, not additional COT events.
- Audit training history for the design actually used. The four primary risk models use direct predictors and a minimum five-year training period, making 2015 the earliest full annual evaluation under a September-2009 start, subject to availability and validation needs. Reconstructions are parallel diagnostics. A later downstream forecast that uses reconstructed positions would still require both training stages (about 2020 under the earlier five-plus-five-year rule). This architectural distinction is declared before results, not used to pick a favorable sample.
- Estimate power and useful-effect thresholds from training data or simulations before opening evaluation results. If the sample cannot distinguish a useful improvement from zero, simplify the secondary claims or describe the study as a pilot. Do not shrink burn-in or change markets after seeing favorable outcomes.
- Defer the physical-tightness interaction and any profit claim until the primary comparison is feasible. Archive prospective forecasts after the protocol is frozen.

**Current conclusion:** the broad original theme overlaps heavily with prior work. The revised contribution tests whether intervening public news substitutes for or complements the position statistic, while separately examining its reconstructibility. This gives the study a clearer economic question and a smaller primary design. Its originality and practical importance still require Proskurin, feasible data and convincing evidence; none is guaranteed.
