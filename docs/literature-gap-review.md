# Literature review: can a reconstructed position replace the official COT report?

**Review date: 19 October 2026 (America/New_York). Research assessment, not an empirical result or a claim of priority.**

## Recommendation

Develop a study of the **decision value of delayed positioning disclosure**: at the same feasible post-publication forecast time, how much energy-futures risk-forecast accuracy is lost when the official position is replaced by a position estimate constructed before publication? Is that loss related to uncertainty measured before the report arrives?

This changes the economic question from whether positioning predicts returns to whether public data can substitute for the disclosure. Accurate position reconstruction and useful risk prediction are different achievements. A reconstruction can miss positions that have no bearing on future risk; conversely, a small reconstruction error can matter during a stressed market.

The reviewed methods leave this as a plausible, narrower contribution. They do **not** establish that nobody has tested it. Two particularly close forecasting articles remain unavailable in full. Resolve those access gaps before making a novelty claim or freezing the final protocol.

## What would make the proposed study more than another forecasting exercise?

1. **A substitution question with a useful null.** Compare a forecast using reported positioning with a separately trained forecast using reconstructed positioning, keeping public features, dates, model family and tuning budgets comparable. Estimate loss from substitution and its uncertainty. A sufficiently tight upper bound could establish practical substitutability for the specified decision; an insignificant coefficient cannot.
2. **A genuine incremental-information check.** Add the actual report to the baseline that already contains its estimate. Also compare with a baseline containing the underlying public predictors directly. An engineered estimate is a function of public information; better performance from that feature alone is not newly discovered information.
3. **One test of when disclosure matters.** Prespecify whether incremental value rises with pre-release reconstruction uncertainty. Uncertainty must use earlier forecast errors, never the current report error. This relationship can fail: uncertain positions may simply be irrelevant to future risk.
4. **Two clocks and original values.** Distinguish the Tuesday the positions describe from the date they become usable. The estimate must target that same Tuesday, not current Friday exposure. Treat shutdown backlogs as batches; repeated maturities and old reports do not create independent announcements.
5. **Decision-specific conclusions.** A daily forecast after the release day measures value that survives the imposed delay. It cannot establish whether the announcement moved prices immediately, whether private traders already knew the positions, or whether the regulator should change disclosure policy.

The [revised working paper](working-paper.md) specifies these comparisons and their limitations. This is an applied contribution in measuring the usefulness of a disclosure, not a proposed new quantile estimator or a causal identification design.

## Alternatives considered

| Direction | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Official report versus public reconstruction, with pre-release uncertainty | Best candidate to develop, conditional on access checks, data coverage and power. It has a clear economic decision and a falsifiable result. |
| COT × inventory tightness for tail forecasts | Retain as a later extension. Considerable overlap with existing positioning/risk/state research; vintage and seasonal-history requirements currently weaken feasibility. |
| Shutdown delays as a natural experiment | Do not use as the main causal design. Delays coincide with unusual market and administrative conditions, involve few episodes and may release multiple weeks together. They are useful audit/stress cases. |
| A more elaborate ML model or a natural-gas-only application | Insufficient by itself. A stronger algorithm or new market does not answer the novelty problem. |

## Feasibility and stop rules before modeling

- Retrieve the two highest-priority full papers and compare their actual experiments with our proposed substitution test. If they already perform it, acknowledge replication and find a demonstrably different question before claiming originality.
- Complete a release/value coverage table by unique publication batch, market and vintage class. The current evidence ledger is not an eligible experiment panel. Do not impute scheduled Fridays as verified release times.
- Validate the six-contract Bloomberg pilot, then acquire the longer panel. Repeated contracts on one date are correlated exposures, not additional COT events.
- Account for **both** training stages. Under a conservative rule using only the post-September-2009 era, five years to train a replica plus five years of chronological replica features for the downstream model pushes the earliest evaluation to about late 2019; complete annual blocks could start in 2020. Uncertainty-calibration history and missing evidence can move it later. Historical backfill can be used in training only after its documented availability, with that choice declared separately.
- Estimate power and useful-effect thresholds from training data or simulations before opening evaluation results. If the sample cannot distinguish a useful improvement from zero, simplify the secondary claims or describe the study as a pilot. Do not shrink burn-in or change markets after seeing favorable outcomes.
- Defer the physical-tightness interaction and any profit claim until the primary comparison is feasible. Archive prospective forecasts after the protocol is frozen.

**Current conclusion:** the broad original theme overlaps heavily with existing work. The narrower disclosure-substitution question is defensible as a proposal, with explicit unresolved literature and data gates. No empirical advantage, publication acceptance, or first-in-literature claim is supported yet.
